# Sample — Email Welcome Sequence
[Sample — fictional client, demonstrates style. Proves the EMAIL_COPY lane.]

Client brief: online yoga studio, 3-email welcome series for new free-trial signups, warm encouraging tone, goal = convert trial to paid membership.

---

## Email 1 — sent immediately
**Subject:** you're in — here's where to start
**Preview:** one 15-minute class, today.

Hey [name],

Welcome in. You don't need to carve out an hour or touch your toes to your forehead — start with one 15-minute class today. That's it.

[Start your first class →]

Most people feel the difference after the first week, not the first session, so the only goal right now is: show up once. We'll take it from there.

See you on the mat,
[Studio]

---

## Email 2 — sent day 3
**Subject:** the secret isn't flexibility
**Preview:** it's the 15 minutes, not the splits.

Hey [name],

Quick myth to kill: you don't get good at yoga and then feel better. You feel better, and the rest follows. The flexibility, the calm, the sleep — those come from just showing up, a little, often.

If you've done a class already — nice. If not, here's a gentle one to ease in:

[Try "Slow Start" (15 min) →]

Your trial's still going. No rush, no pressure. Just roll out the mat.

[Studio]

---

## Email 3 — sent day 6 (trial ending)
**Subject:** your trial ends tomorrow (don't lose your streak)
**Preview:** keep going for less than a coffee a week.

Hey [name],

Your free week wraps up tomorrow. If the mat's started to feel like a good part of your day, here's the easy part: membership is [$X/month] — unlimited classes, anytime, less than a coffee a week.

[Keep my access →]

And if now's not the time, no hard feelings — you can pick it back up whenever. Either way, I'm glad you gave it a shot.

Breathe easy,
[Studio]

*Note: each email has one job and one CTA. Email 1 removes friction, email 2 reframes the objection ("I'm not flexible"), email 3 converts on a soft deadline. No guilt, on-brand calm.*
